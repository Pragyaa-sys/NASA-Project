☉ The Solar System

Hover over any world — don't click, just linger — to see what makes it unique.

The Sun

Our star holds about 99.8% of the entire solar system's mass. It's a giant ball of hydrogen and helium fusing at its core, and light from its surface takes roughly 8 minutes to reach Earth.

Mercury

The smallest and innermost planet, Mercury has almost no atmosphere to hold in heat, so its surface swings from scorching days to freezing nights. A year here lasts just 88 Earth days.

Venus

Wrapped in thick, toxic clouds, Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system thanks to a runaway greenhouse effect. Oddly, its day is longer than its year.

Earth

Our home, and the only known world with liquid water on its surface and life. It's also the only planet in the solar system not named after a mythological god.

Mars

The Red Planet gets its color from iron oxide dust on its surface. It's home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system, standing nearly three times the height of Everest.

Jupiter

The largest planet by far, Jupiter is a swirling giant of gas with a storm — the Great Red Spot — big enough to swallow Earth whole. Over 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.

Saturn

Famous for its dazzling rings made of ice and rock, Saturn is so light it would actually float in water, if you could find a bathtub large enough.

Uranus

This ice giant rotates almost completely on its side, likely the result of an ancient collision, so it essentially rolls around the Sun rather than spinning upright.

Neptune

The most distant planet is also the windiest, with gusts clocked at over 2,100 km/h — the fastest in the solar system. Its deep blue color comes from methane in its atmosphere.

✦ The Moons

Hover over any moon — don't click, just linger — to see which world it belongs to.

The Moon

Moon of Earth

Earth's only natural satellite formed roughly 4.5 billion years ago, likely from debris after a Mars-sized body struck the young Earth. Its gravity is why our oceans have tides.

Phobos

Moon of Mars

The larger of Mars's two lumpy, crater-covered moons, Phobos orbits so close to the planet that it circles Mars three times a day, and it's slowly spiraling inward.

Io

Moon of Jupiter

The most volcanically active body in the solar system, Io is squeezed and stretched by Jupiter's immense gravity, erupting molten rock hundreds of kilometers into space.

Titan

Moon of Saturn

Saturn's largest moon is the only moon with a thick atmosphere, and the only place besides Earth known to have rivers, lakes, and rain — made of liquid methane instead of water.

Triton

Moon of Neptune

Neptune's largest moon orbits backward compared to Neptune's rotation, a strong hint that it was once a captured world from the icy Kuiper Belt rather than born alongside its planet.

Charon

Moon of Pluto

Charon is so large relative to Pluto that the two don't quite orbit like a planet and moon — they orbit a shared point in space between them, more like a double world.
